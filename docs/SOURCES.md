# Regulatory sources

Every plate scheme in `metadata/**/*.yaml` cites its legal basis through one or
more `sources[].id` entries. This document is the registry those IDs resolve
to. A validation result carries the stable `scheme.id`, which identifies the
YAML scheme and, through its `sources` list, the regulation that defines the
format — the legal citations themselves live here, not in the API.

> **Scope of these sources.** They document the _format, series and visual
> characteristics_ of plates as prescribed by regulation. They do **not**
> establish whether a given plate has been issued or assigned — libplate makes
> no such claim (see the notice in the README).

**Maintenance.** Registration rules change and consolidated official texts are
updated in place. Each source records when it was last checked. Before every
metadata release, re-verify the sources touched by the changed schemes.

```
sourceCheckedAt: 2026-07-27
metadataVersion:  2026.07.27
```

### Spain

The consolidated BOE texts are served block by block through the open-data API
(`https://www.boe.es/datosabiertos/api/legislacion-consolidada/id/{BOE-ID}/texto/bloque/{block}`),
which is the efficient way to re-verify `ES-RGV`: the diplomatic-regime text is
block `i-4`, the colour/dimension table is block `cuadro1`. Each block returns
every version — take the latest `fecha_vigencia`. Note that the RGV deliberately
fixes no digit widths for the diplomatic series (they come from `ES-DGT-TYPES`),
and that the Orden de 31 de mayo de 1977 (BOE-A-1977-13514) is _derogada_ and
adds nothing, so it is deliberately not cited.

### Germany

The district-code list is **no longer an annex of the FZV**: the FZV of 2011 was
replaced by the FZV of 20 July 2023, under which the BMV sets the codes per
§ 9(3) and the consolidated list is the KBA publication. To refresh
`metadata/tables/de-unterscheidungszeichen.json`, re-download the KBA Faltblatt
and parse pages 3-11 (codes approved for assignment) plus pages 12-13 (revoked
codes, still valid until deregistration). The listing is alphabetical with Ä/Ö/Ü
collated as A/O/U, which doubles as a completeness check.

### Lithuania

Unusually for this registry, **one article carries every composition**. Point 21
of the registration rules enumerates the letter/digit arrangement for each
vehicle category exhaustively, so no drawing, standard or agency practice has to
be leaned on the way Estonia, Romania or Czechia do. The consolidated text is
served by `e-seimas.lrs.lt`; the HTML page is a JavaScript shell, so fetch the
edition PDF instead — read `href="/rs/actualedition/<docId>/<editionId>/…"` out
of the `/asr` page and request that path with `/format/ISO_PDF/`.

Two traps, both checked 2026-07-26. The rules were **wholly re-issued** by the
redaction of order Nr. 1V-664 (2024-11-08), which is why the consolidated text
carries almost no per-point amendment annotations and why the introduction date

### Ireland

Everything hangs off **one regulation that has been substituted twice**. S.I.
No. 318 of 1992 is the principal instrument, but its Regulation 9 (the
composition) and its First Schedule (the plate and the index-mark tables) as
they stand today are the text put there by S.I. No. 452 of 2013 — which also
_revoked_ the corresponding provision of S.I. No. 542 of 2012 (its Regulation 6).
So: quote the composition from the 2013 instrument, cite the 1992 one for the
First Schedule paragraphs it did not touch, and cite the 2012 one only for the
introduction of the half-year numeral. There is no consolidated text — the Irish
Statute Book publishes revised versions of Acts, not of statutory instruments,
so each amendment has to be read on top of the principal instrument by hand.

Two things the Regulations do **not** contain, and no amount of reading will
find: any width for the sequence number, and any series other than the ordinary
one and `ZV` — no diplomatic, trade, military or temporary mark appears in them
at all. `IE-REVENUE-ZZ-ZV` is therefore the only authority for the `ZZ` export
series, at the same evidence rank as the Norwegian Skiltserier page.

`User-Agent` and the `/print` view returns the whole instrument as one page:

**The sequence width, and why the geometry cannot supply one.** Reg. 9 gives the
sequence number no digit count, so the Romanian move — deriving the width from
the plate's character capacity — is the obvious fallback. It does not work here.
Paragraph 16 leaves 470 mm between the EU band and the border; paragraph 14
gives each character "a total width of the space taken" of 36 mm and each gap
"not less than 8 mm". Read as glyph width plus gap, a one-line plate holds four
sequence digits behind a one-letter index mark — which every `191-D-12345` on
the road contradicts. Read as 36 mm already absorbing the gap, the same plate
holds six. libplate takes the permissive reading and bounds the sequence at six
digits, and that bound is **libplate's, not the Regulations'**.

1. Add a row here (or a `###` heading for a heavily-used primary source) with a
   stable ID, the full legal reference, what it covers, and a URL.
2. Reference the ID from the scheme YAML under `sources`, with a `section` when
   the document is long (e.g. `Anexo XVIII, I.A.c`).

> Do not base a scheme on photographs, blogs or unofficial listings. Every
